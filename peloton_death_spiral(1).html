☠ The Framework — UNC Kenan-Flagler Death Spiral Theory

Demand-Driven Spiral

Customer demand drops → lower volume → fixed costs spread over fewer units → higher unit cost → price pressure → more defections. Peloton's subscriber loss is textbook demand-driven. Each departing subscriber forces remaining members to absorb more of the ~$550M fixed cost base.

The Telecom Analogy

Like the internal telecom example: 2,000 lines at $500 each → 1,000 leave → price jumps to $800 → more defections. Peloton's equivalent: 3.11M subs at $44/mo → subscribers flee → fixed cost per remaining sub rises → platform value perception erodes.

Practical Capacity Defense

Using practical capacity overhead rates (max capacity, not actual) prevents the spiral by keeping unit costs stable regardless of volume fluctuations. Peloton must avoid letting actual subscriber counts drive cost allocation — or the spiral becomes self-fulfilling.

The Churn Lever

At 1.9% monthly churn (Q2 FY2026), Peloton loses ~54,700 subscribers per month. LTV collapses: at $44 ARPU and 69% margin, LTV = $44 × 0.69 ÷ 0.019 = $1,599. If churn hits 3%, LTV drops to $1,012 — a 37% destruction of subscriber value.

Supply-Side Risk

Peloton's 11% workforce layoff (2026) and hardware line cuts are supply-driven spiral triggers. Cutting content creators or instructors reduces platform value → accelerates churn → fewer subs to absorb fixed costs → more cuts required. The Manic Broadcasting trap.

The Zero-CM Trap

Like Clay Sprays acquiring Coronas: adding app subscribers at near-zero contribution margin lowers average fixed cost but doesn't improve profitability. Peloton's free/cheap app tiers may be masking the true cost burden on connected fitness subscribers.
